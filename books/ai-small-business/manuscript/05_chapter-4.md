# Chapter 4: The Message Engine (Email, DMs, Reviews, Complaints)

Open your sent folder and count. Not the total — the kinds. Across a month, a small business's outbound messages fall into a depressingly short list: the enquiry reply, the quote follow-up, the scheduling message, the "running late" update, the invoice nudge, the review response, the complaint response, the "just checking you got this." Eight kinds, maybe ten.

That is good news hiding inside the drudgery. Ten kinds of message is a closed set, which means it can be handled properly — each one designed once, drafted in seconds thereafter, reviewed by you in the fifteen seconds from Chapter 3, and sent with the confidence that comes from having thought about it the first time instead of the two hundredth.

## The Enquiry Reply

This is the highest-value message in most businesses, because it is the first impression and it is answered while the customer is still comparing you to two other tabs. The economics are brutal and simple: reply in five minutes and you are often the only one who bothered; reply tomorrow and you are the third quote in a stack.

The workflow that works looks like this. Keep a document — or a note in your phone — with your standard enquiry facts: what you do, where, typical prices or ranges, lead times, how onboarding works, the next step. When an enquiry arrives, paste it into your AI tool, paste your facts, and ask for a reply under 150 words in your tone that answers their actual question and proposes one specific next step.

Their actual question, not the generic one. That is why you paste their message rather than describing it. The model can see that they mentioned a wedding date in June, or that they are asking about dog-friendliness, or that they wrote at eleven at night in a hurry — and it can respond to that specific thing, which is the entire difference between "thank you for your interest" and a reply that feels human.

The specific next step matters just as much. "Reply with two times that suit you this week" beats "let me know if you have questions," every time, and it is the kind of discipline that is easy to forget at 4:45pm and easy to remember when a template does it for you. The model will gladly propose one if you ask; pick the version that matches how you actually like to work.

Expect to edit the first two or three of these heavily while you tune your fact sheet and tone example. By the tenth, you will be changing a word or two. That trajectory — heavy edit to light edit — is what setting up a task properly looks like, and it is the reason the audit's baseline numbers matter.

## Review Responses

Reviews are a small business's most underworked asset, and the reason is always the same: writing them is tedious and the payoff is delayed. AI removes the tedium entirely.

Five stars with a paragraph of praise: ask for a warm, specific, twenty-five-word thank you that mentions the service they named. Do not gush. Named specifics in the reply ("glad the shelving came together the way you wanted") signal to the next reader that a real person answered.

Four stars with a real complaint: this one gets Chapter 9 treatment too, but the shape is: acknowledge what they said specifically, no defensiveness, one sentence of what you are doing about it, and an invitation to continue offline with a contact detail. Ask the model for a draft that does all four and contains no words from the corporate apology lexicon — "we apologise for any inconvenience" should be treated as a red-flag string and deleted on sight.

Three stars or below with facts you dispute: draft the reply, then sleep on it. Models will happily write a polite, meticulously reasoned rebuttal that is completely correct and will still make you look like a business that argues with customers in public. Sometimes the right response is a short, warm invitation to contact you, full stop. The model can produce that in one line; your job is knowing when one line is the answer.

The compound effect matters more than any individual reply. Fifty consistently answered reviews across two years is a marketing asset that costs an hour of total effort once AI handles the typing, and it influences every future customer who is deciding between you and the alternative.

## Complaints and Difficult Replies

The hardest messages to write are the ones where you are tired, defensive, or upset — which is exactly when you most need a draft that is not shaped by the mood you are in.

The technique: write what you actually want to say first, unfiltered, in the AI chat — do not send it, it is a scratchpad, not the output — then ask for a version that is professional, acknowledges their point without admitting fault we have not established, and proposes a resolution. What comes back is usually the version you would have written after a night's sleep, in four seconds.

Two firm rules for this category. First, you read the customer's message into the conversation yourself, accurately and fairly, including the parts that are reasonable — if you summarize an angry email through your own filter, the draft will answer a strawman and inflame things. Second, any reply involving money, liability, a refund beyond your normal policy, or the words "legal" or "unsafe" does not get sent the same day, model-drafted or not. Use AI to understand your position and prepare the question you will ask your accountant or insurer, not to settle it.

There is a deeper point buried in the workflow. Businesses do not lose customers primarily over the initial mistake; they lose them over the response to it. A complaint handled with speed and specificity — the exact thing this workflow gives you, because drafting is no longer the bottleneck — is routinely converted into the most loyal customer you have. That conversion has always been possible. The reason it does not happen more often is that the reply sits in the queue behind everything else. Remove the queue and watch what happens to your recovery rate.

## The Multi-Channel Mess

Most owners are answering the same customer across three channels — email, Instagram or WhatsApp, maybe a marketplace or booking platform — and re-explaining themselves each time. AI will not unify your channels, and any tool claiming to is usually a subscription you do not need. But it will do something better for free: it will hold the thread.

When a conversation hops channels, paste the two or three relevant messages in and ask for a one-paragraph recap plus a reply that acknowledges where things got lost. Customers experience this as remarkable attentiveness — "sorry, I lost the thread when we moved to WhatsApp, you were saying the Tuesday delivery worked?" — when in fact you pasted three texts into a box and asked for the gap to be found.

For the truly busy channels, there is a useful intermediate step before any fancy automation: a triage prompt. Paste the day's unread messages and ask for a list sorted into reply-now (problems, money, time-sensitive), reply-today (enquiries, questions), and reply-when-you-can (everything else), with a one-line suggested response for each. What used to be an inbox trawl becomes a fifteen-minute pass over a ranked list. This single workflow is, for a lot of owners, where the first two hours of the week come from.

## Building Your Message Library

After a month of this you will have a pile of sent messages that work: the enquiry reply that got a yes, the complaint response that turned a one-star into an update, the follow-up that closed. Do not let them evaporate.

Keep one file — a plain document, no app — with a heading per message type and two or three of your best real examples under each. This is now your most valuable operational asset. It is what makes the drafts better, it is what trains a new employee in an afternoon, and it is what you feed the model when you want consistency across two people answering the same channel.

The examples have to be real ones you sent and stand behind, not theoretically good ones. The gap between "what we would say" and "what we did say, and it worked" is where a lot of customer communication goes to die. Yours will not, because you are collecting the ones that shipped.

## This Week

Pick your two highest-volume message types from the audit — for most owners, the enquiry reply and the review response. Build the fact sheet if you have not (fifteen minutes, once), find one real example of each message you are proud of, and run five real ones through the workflow this week. Count the minutes saved against your baseline on the sticky note, and note honestly how many you would have sent unedited. That last number — trust in the draft — is the one that tells you whether this is working.
